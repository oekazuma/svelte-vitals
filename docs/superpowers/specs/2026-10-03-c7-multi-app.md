# C7 (per-rule precision) judges a rule only when its false positives come from two or more apps

Status: decided.

## Problem

C7 in `2026-09-24-v1-release-criteria.md` requires 90% precision of every rule with at least 10
holdout findings. Holdouts 1–17 failed it seventeen times. In the recent rounds it is the criterion
that decides the result: holdout 13 failed only C7 (C8 is no longer a gate), and holdouts 16 and 17
failed it on rules whose false positives all came from one app.

A holdout is 14 apps, so most rules land between 10 and 30 findings. One app's idiom then decides a
rule: holdout 13's `a11y/id-duplication` (one app's URL-decided layout), holdout 16's
`seo/heading-level-skip` (duitgee, 2 of 14 findings), holdout 17's `a11y/id-duplication` and
`seo/heading-level-skip` (ja-automation-platform, 9 false positives between them). Each of those
classes was fixed in the round after, as every class is; the next holdout brings another app with
another idiom.

## Decision

1. **C7 judges a rule with at least 10 holdout findings whose `fp` come from 2 or more apps.** Its
   precision must stay at or above 90%, as before.
2. **A rule below 90% whose false positives all come from one app is published in the result, with
   the app, and does not decide it.** Its class is fixed after the round like any other.

The other nine criteria are unchanged. Results of holdouts 1–17 stand as published; the definition
applies from holdout 18, whose result also reports C7 under the old definition.

## What still decides a result

- A rule broken on several apps fails C7, as before. Holdouts 14 and 15 would still fail it (3 rules
  each).
- A class shared by two apps fails C6, whatever the rule.
- Every false positive, single-app or not, counts in C4 and C5, and any false critical fails C3.

What no longer decides a result is one app pulling one rule under 90% by a class no other app has.

## Alternatives measured

Rules failing C7 on holdouts 13–17 under each definition, from the committed verdicts:

| Definition                                     | H13 | H14 | H15 | H16 | H17 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Current: ≥ 10 findings                         | 1   | 4   | 5   | 1   | 2   |
| **≥ 10 findings, `fp` from 2 or more apps**    | 0   | 3   | 3   | 0   | 0   |
| ≥ 20 findings                                  | 1   | 4   | 4   | 0   | 0   |
| ≥ 10 findings, the rule seen in 3 or more apps | 1   | 4   | 4   | 1   | 2   |
| Pooled over the last 3 holdouts, ≥ 30 findings | 3   | 2   | 4   | 6   | 4   |

Raising the minimum count leaves more rules unjudged without asking where their errors come from, and
still fails holdout 13 on one app. Requiring the rule to appear in several apps does not separate
the cases, because a rule usually fires on most apps while its false positives sit in one. Pooling
carries classes already fixed into later rounds. Counting the apps the false positives come from
asks the question C6 asks of a class, per rule.
